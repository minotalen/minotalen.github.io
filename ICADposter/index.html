GlucoSound

A glucose sensor reports a value every five minutes. The trend chart built from those values is what keeps a person safe, and in the manufacturers' apps it is invisible to a screen reader. This app maps it to pitch instead.

Glucose to pitch · linear · one value, one frequency

No compression anywhere in the scale

A strict bijection: the same value always produces the same pitch. Widening the in-range band would gain resolution where readings matter least, and break the property that makes the scale learnable.

Span and octave are user-set

Narrow 393 Hz, normal 654, wide 1046, plus an octave shift. Hearing loss is common alongside diabetes, so a fixed span would hand some listeners a range they cannot fully hear.

Chart view  ·  reading a curve without seeing it

The chart is hidden. Twelve columns take its place.

The visible chart is removed from the accessibility tree entirely. In its place the window is cut into twelve equal time slices, each one a separate VoiceOver element. Swipe left and right to step through them.

  • Each column is summarised for glucose, not described as generic data
  • Values, shape, then time. The order is a setting
  • Dates are relative: yesterday, or the weekday within the last six days
  • Swiping past either edge pages to the next window and lands focus on the adjacent column

Explore  ·  the curve as continuous sound

Hold, drag, lift.
  • A finger on the curve holds a tone. It sounds until you lift
  • Drag to scrub. Pitch follows the value continuously with no noticeable delay in audio.
  • Hour boundaries fire a short click as temporal landmarks
  • On lift, value and time are spoken
  • Six instruments, additive synthesis, made to support repeat listening in public

Full depth as soon as the line is crossed

The cue does not grow with the size of the excursion. It reports the boundary, so a value just past the threshold sounds the same as one far past it. Reduced hearing is common alongside diabetes, and a cue at fixed depth stays available to more listeners.

Crossings are also felt

Two pulses on entering an out-of-range zone, one on returning. A crossing stays legible while scrubbing fast, when a change in modulation could be missed.

Three zones, three states of one tone
Above 180 vibrato, ±35 cents at 5 Hz
70 to 180 clean, no modulation
Below 70 tremolo, amplitude modulated at 6 Hz

Shape-led narration happens only above confidence 0.45. The wording turns definitive only above 0.70, and below that it hedges inside the sentence rather than in a footnote. Below the coverage threshold the narration changes again. A confident summary of a half-empty window is the worst output available.

Under the hood

Synthesis and speech,
on one device, at the same time.

Real-time synthesis and VoiceOver both want the audio hardware, and getting them to share it was a headache that required lots of tinkering.

Speech on its own serial queue

Every utterance is dispatched to a dedicated queue. Text-to-speech goes out over inter-process calls that can stall, and on the main thread a stall is a frozen interface.

The audio session is handed over, not fought over

When VoiceOver is switched on, the engine stops and the session is released to it. Interruptions from calls or Siri, and headphone route changes, restart the engine, except while VoiceOver is running, where restarting would compete with it.

Session setup deferred out of launch

Configuring and activating the audio session forces a synchronous round-trip to the media server. Done during the first render it hung the app on launch, so it now happens lazily, in the background, before explore mode is first needed.

A render thread that allocates nothing

The synthesis callback captures no objects, takes no locks and touches no reference counting. Per-sample state is shadowed in 32-bit floats for the inner loop and written back once per buffer. A lock here would risk priority inversion, and a dropout is a hole in the data.

Audibility at the bottom

Hypoglycemia lives at the bottom of the range, where the speaker and the ear are both weakest. Below 350 Hz the third, fourth and fifth harmonics are mixed in, and the ear rebuilds the fundamental it never received.

Why vibrato is set in cents

Pitch is heard as ratios, so a deviation fixed in hertz shrinks into nothing as the tone rises. The cue faded out exactly where glucose was highest. Set in cents it is the same size everywhere.
